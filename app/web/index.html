How a listing gets in

  1. Somebody submits it A resident, a business owner or a field worker puts in a business, the district it sits in, and what it does or sells.
  2. An operator goes and checks Somebody confirms the premises exist and that the description is fair. Claims that cannot be confirmed are sent back rather than softened.
  3. It goes live and the gaps start moving The record appears in the directory, and every search for something nearby feeds the gap analysis, so the map of what Kampala is missing stays current.

Only verified records reach this page. Anything still waiting on a decision sits in the verification queue and is counted nowhere the public can see. The other half of the directory — the ranked list of what Kampala is missing — is one click away, and every card on it carries the sentence the engine ranked it by.

What it will not tell you

Every search is recorded as an anonymised demand signal, and those signals are what the gap analysis is built from. Aggregates computed over fewer than — distinct people are withheld rather than rounded, so a handful of searches can never be subtracted back down to an individual. The engine knows the answer and says it is not publishable yet. That is the whole point of running it locally.

Endpoints

The directory is a replaceable interface over the engine. Everything the pages above do is a plain HTTP call, so you can build your own on top of it.

  • /api/v1/locations Every administrative level, filterable with ?kind=ward
  • /api/v1/categories The category tree, with the skills linked to each node
  • /api/v1/search?q=… Full-text search. Records an anonymised demand signal
  • /api/v1/insights/opportunities Ranked gaps, subject to the k-anonymity floor
  • /api/v1/requests What people are looking for, posted without an account
  • /api/v1/meta Version, configuration, limits and current counts

Working on the directory yourself? Sign in to the verification queue. Accounts are created by an admin; there is no public sign-up.
